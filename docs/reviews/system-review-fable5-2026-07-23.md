# Review: Model-collaboration study — plan and code (round 7)

**Reviewer:** Claude Fable 5 (fresh context)
**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Artifacts reviewed:** `CLAUDE.md`; `docs/research/experimental-design.md`; `docs/decisions.md`; `docs/status.md`; `docs/research/calibration-findings.md`; `docs/research/power-analysis.md`; `src/executor/{api_client.py,interpreter.py,runner.py}`; `src/experiment/{phase1.py,spec.py,prompts.py}`; `src/experiment/benchmarks/livecodebench.py`; `src/protocols/{conditions.py,reconcile.py}`; `scripts/run_pilot.py`; `tests/` (ran offline: 159 passed).

## Summary assessment

The design is unusually disciplined — the macro-model framing, the selector-as-oracle rule, dollars-as-compute, and the pre-registered interaction/fallback structure are all sound, and the code faithfully expresses them with clean ground-truth hermeticity. The most important issue is that today's Gemini "fix" treats a specific symptom (Gemini truncation) rather than the class of bug, and the class almost certainly still bites a second subject: **the harness inspects no provider's finish-reason, and gpt-5.4-mini is a GPT-5-family reasoning model capped at 4096 output tokens including its reasoning — so gpt may be silently truncating on hard LCB tasks exactly as Gemini was, which would mean the `best_model` anchor and the cross-model re-ranking the pilot is meant to settle are both contaminated in a way nobody has measured.**

## Specific findings

1. **No truncation / finish-reason detection on any provider; the Gemini fix is a threshold bump, not a fix for the class. (High)** `api_client.py` reads token counts but never inspects `stop_reason` (Anthropic, `_call_anthropic` 273-301), `choices[0].finish_reason` (OpenAI, `_call_openai` 303-335), or `candidates[0].finish_reason` (Google, `_call_google` 368-413). The grep for `finish_reason|stop_reason` across `src/` returns nothing. A response cut off by the length cap is non-empty, passes the `text.strip()` guard (line 456), records `status="success"`, and scores 0 downstream — indistinguishable from a wrong answer. This is precisely how the pre-fix Gemini call scored 0.00. The comment at `_call_google` 381-382 claims a budget-exhausting call "scores as a capability failure of the bounded harness, documented rather than hidden" — but nothing detects or documents it; it is hidden exactly as before, just at 32768 instead of 4096. **Why it matters:** gpt-5.4-mini reasons by default and OpenAI counts reasoning tokens against `max_completion_tokens` (=4096 here). On LCB/hard, where gpt calibrated at 0.25 strict / 0.48 frac, some of those "failures" could be truncations, not capability failures — the same artifact that made Gemini look like 0.08. The "gpt/haiku sit well under their caps (~2200)" note in `status.md` measures token *count*, not `finish_reason==length`, and for OpenAI the relevant number is total completion tokens *including reasoning*. **Direction:** read the finish/stop reason on all three providers; classify length-truncation as its own status (retry with more budget, or at minimum flag it in telemetry) rather than letting it score as a capability failure; and re-check the gpt/haiku hard-subset truncation rate before trusting any per-subject rate.

2. **The 8× Google output budget violates the design's own "same max output length" constraint and confounds the pilot's cross-model comparison. (High)** `_call_google` sets `max_output_tokens = 8 * self.max_tokens` = 32768 (`api_client.py:385`) while OpenAI/Anthropic use 4096. `experimental-design.md` § "Common Interface Constraints" explicitly requires "Same max output length (`max_tokens` pinned across providers)." The asymmetry maps directly onto vendor default-reasoning behavior: Anthropic (haiku) doesn't think by default so 4096 is ample; OpenAI reasons into the 4096 cap (may truncate); Gemini now has 8× headroom (won't). So a pilot result of "gemini > gpt" is partly an artifact of gpt being throttled and gemini not. **Why it matters:** this is the exact re-ranking question the pilot's Condition-A columns are supposed to settle, and it is confounded by an uncontrolled harness resource. It is also not recorded in `docs/decisions.md` (only as a "named harness property" in `status.md`), unlike every other measurement-affecting choice in this project. **Direction:** record it as a decision; and rather than an ad-hoc per-vendor multiplier, give every provider enough total budget that neither reasoning nor answer truncates, then report each vendor's realized thinking spend as a measured behavior (it is already billed correctly into dollars).

3. **In the pilot, compute-matching holds only for D′ vs B — not for D, C, or E. (High)** `build_conditions` (`run_pilot.py` 136-187) runs one B at n=8, dollar-matched to D′; D/C/E run at their own realized costs, with D ~6× D′ (Gemini thinking). The design's Best-of-N discipline requires a heterogeneous protocol spending $kX to be compared to single-model repeat-and-select at $kX. So the pilot supports a matched-budget read only for the D′/B pair (and even that is cap-not-exact, N=86, single seed). **Why it matters:** "beats the compute-matched single model" — the load-bearing claim — is not evaluable for D, C, or E from this run. **Direction:** for a pilot, lean on the design's *own* headline metric, dollars-per-solved-task, which is cost-fair by construction regardless of realized spend, instead of raw success-rate comparisons; if a matched success-rate claim for D is wanted, add a second B whose n matches D's realized per-task cost.

4. **`ParScore` parse-failure falls back to a hardcoded 0.5, not an AST policy. (Medium)** `interpreter.py` 564-572 substitutes `0.5` on a score parse failure, whereas `PickOne` routes parse failure through the AST-level `ParseFailurePolicy` (592-618) added by the 2026-04-19 decision. This is on the live D/D′ path (`reconcile.py` uses `par_score` + `weighted_vote`). 0.5 is not a neutral fallback — it fabricates mid-confidence that can outrank a draft that legitimately scored 0.4, and a systematic parse failure gives every draft 0.5 → tie → seeded-random selection, collapsing ReConcile's confidence weighting silently (it is counted in telemetry, but the *value* is baked in). **Direction:** give `ParScore` the same AST-level parse-failure policy `PickOne` has; the 2026-04-19 decision's stated lesson ("any executor-level fallback that affects measurement belongs at the AST") applies here and was left half-applied.

5. **Truncated-code / fence-extraction interaction makes truncation invisible as truncation. (Medium)** `_extract_code` (`livecodebench.py` 98-113) requires a *closing* ``` ``` ```; a truncated response with an unclosed fence yields no regex match and falls back to running the entire response (prose + partial code) as Python → runtime error → 0.0. This is the concrete mechanism by which the Gemini truncation scored 0.00 and stayed invisible. It compounds finding 1: without finish-reason checking, a length-truncated answer is scored identically to a genuine wrong answer. **Direction:** finding 1's fix resolves this; separately, consider logging when the fallback path (no fence) is taken, since for a fenced-output prompt that path is itself a truncation/format signal.

6. **The `best_model` anchor is unsettled on two axes, not one. (Medium)** The pilot keeps `best_model=gpt-5.4-mini` "per the recorded calibration." But that calibration under-measured Gemini (now fixed) *and* may have under-measured gpt (finding 1, unfixed). The n=3 smoke showing Gemini 3/3 is not evidence to re-anchor, and neither is the old calibration evidence to keep the anchor — both are contaminated by the same class of bug. Haiku is the only subject whose calibration is clearly clean (no default reasoning). **Direction:** before re-anchoring, re-run the full Condition-A calibration on the fixed harness *with truncation detection for all three providers* on the medium+hard pool; only then decide `best_model`. Note C's judge and E's meta-reviewer bake in `best_model` structurally, so a re-anchor is a rebuild of those conditions, not a relabel.

7. **No in-repo spend guard; no budget-cap enforcement. (Low)** `runner.py` documents that it does not enforce `BudgetTier` caps, and `run_pilot.py` has none either; the `BudgetTier` field on each `ConditionSpec` is decorative in the pilot. The $115 watchdog referenced in `status.md` is not in the committed code (grep finds nothing), so it is an external wrapper I cannot review. **Direction:** fine for a disclosed pilot, but note the only spend guard is out-of-repo; an in-process cumulative-dollar abort is cheap insurance for a ~22h unattended run where one vendor's thinking spend is already the dominant, most variable cost.

8. **"Variance across seeds" is not achievable in the current harness. (Low)** No generation seed is passed to any provider (grep confirms; OpenAI supports a `seed` param). The interpreter seed controls only tie-break / parse-fallback RNG (`interpreter.py:121`), not generation; generation stochasticity comes from vendor-default temperature. Re-running with `--seed 0` reproduces tie-breaks but not generations. **Direction:** don't list seed-variance as a Phase 1 metric until generation is seedable where the API allows it; report it as a known limitation for the others.

9. **Hermeticity is clean (positive, verified).** The LCB query template carries only `question_content` + public examples (`livecodebench.py` 74-90, 315-324); private tests never enter the prompt; scoring runs in a scrubbed-env subprocess that drops every `*_API_KEY` (`_scrubbed_env` 152-167). Aggregation is internal to every macro-model and the executable evaluator is strictly downstream — the selector-as-oracle rule holds in code. This is the part prior reviewers scrutinized hardest and it stands up.

**Design-level blind spot (shared with prior generations).** Dollars-as-compute was chosen because dollars compose across tokenizers — but they do not compose across *hidden reasoning*. Vendor default-reasoning is simultaneously a cost (captured in dollars) and a capability lever (not captured, and asymmetrically throttled by the shared output cap). A "heterogeneity helps" result on a pool containing one heavy-hidden-reasoning member is not cleanly separable from "one pool member does a lot of invisible reasoning that the cap happens to permit for its vendor." This is bigger than the pilot; it should be named as a first-class confound for Phase 1 proper, alongside the price-arbitrage confound the design already names so carefully.

## Answers to the four posed questions

1. **Middle-band recalibration.** None of the three options is safe until the harness is trusted, because the current calibration is contaminated for *two* subjects, not just Gemini (finding 1/6). Step zero: re-run full Condition-A calibration on the fixed harness with all-provider truncation detection. Then note the consequence — if post-fix Gemini really lands ~0.90 on the medium+hard pool, that pool is the pool's *easy* band, where the utility curve predicts collaboration *degrades*; running the confirmatory test there measures the wrong region. So the right move is **(b) expand the pool and re-stratify against fixed-harness, best-subject-verified rates**, then re-freeze the band before any confirmatory run. **(c)** (re-anchor to Gemini, keep the pool) leaves you measuring in the easy band; **(a)** (harder-subset selection) is fine mechanically but must run against the fixed harness, not the old numbers. Effect on the pre-registered fallback test: the band boundaries were selected for a now-wrong best subject, so the pre-registration no longer corresponds to 45–55% for the true best subject — re-selection is *required* to keep the fallback test valid, and it must be frozen before the test data are peeked at, or you inherit the calibrate-and-test-on-the-same-data problem.

2. **Vendor thinking spend — compute, or vendor behavior?** Under the design's own committed unit it is unambiguously compute: dollars were chosen precisely because "$1 spent is $1 spent regardless of which model consumed it," and thinking tokens are real dollars. So matching must respect it. Matching B to D′ while comparing B to D is therefore internally inconsistent with the design — D is 6× and has no matched single-model baseline. Two coherent resolutions: (i) run a second B matched to D's realized cost (keeps the matched-budget success-rate comparison but is expensive), or (ii) restrict success-rate matched claims to D′/B and report D/C/E on **dollars-per-solved-task**, the design's headline efficiency metric, which is cost-fair by construction and is exactly the lens that answers "is D's extra spend worth it." I'd take (ii) for the pilot and reserve (i) for the confirmatory phase. What you should *not* do is reclassify thinking as "a behavior to report alongside" and thereby quietly switch compute units mid-experiment — that breaks the pre-registration.

3. **8× Gemini output budget — confound or acceptable asymmetry?** Confound requiring equalization (finding 2). It violates the design's stated "same max output length" constraint, and the asymmetry lands exactly where it does the most damage — on the vendor-reasoning axis the pilot is trying to measure. "OpenAI/Anthropic use under ~2200 visible" does not clear it, because (a) the risk is `finish_reason==length` on the *hard* subset, not the mean, and (b) for a reasoning model the binding quantity is total completion tokens including reasoning. Equalize by giving all providers enough headroom that neither reasoning nor answer truncates, verify truncation rate ≈ 0 per provider on the hard subset, and report realized thinking spend as measured (and dollar-billed) behavior.

4. **What would be overclaims for a public write-up of N=86.** At N=86, single seed, one benchmark, one difficulty pool: (a) any statistical claim — the interaction test has ~0.15 power and the middle-band test ~0.30 at N≈100 per your own power analysis; report descriptive numbers only, no p-values or "significant." (b) Any "beats the compute-matched single model" claim except, weakly, D′ vs B — and even that is cap-not-exact and underpowered. (c) Any "heterogeneity helps" claim from D vs D′ — descriptive only, and confounded by D's 6× spend. (d) Any per-stratum or utility-curve claim — the strata aren't populated at power. (e) Any statement of which single model is "best" — the anchor is unsettled by the truncation confound on two of three vendors. (f) Even "collaboration helps on LCB" is shaky, because the pool may be the *easy* band for the true best subject, where the hypothesis predicts no help or degradation. What you *can* honestly claim: the harness runs the full matrix end-to-end; realized per-condition costs and dollars-per-solved-task on this pool; the truncation finding itself (a genuine methodological result worth writing up); and qualitative observations flagged as hypothesis-generating.

## Recommendation

**Revise and re-review.** The design is fundamentally sound and the pilot is fine to run and report as an explicitly descriptive, hypothesis-generating artifact under the claim limits in question 4. But before any pre-registered Phase 1 run, the harness needs finding 1 (all-provider truncation detection) and finding 2 (output-budget symmetry) fixed, finding 4 (ParScore parse policy) closed, and a fixed-harness recalibration (findings 1/6) that settles `best_model` and re-freezes the band. Those are targeted, in-scope fixes — not a rethink — but they are load-bearing for measurement integrity, and two of them (truncation, budget asymmetry) are live in the run happening right now.
