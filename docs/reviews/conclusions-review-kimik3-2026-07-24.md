## Summary assessment

Every headline number in the doc reproduces from the raw log to within Monte-Carlo noise — the descriptive core is solid and the framing caveats are unusually honest. The problems are in the interpretive layer: the "matched compute" phrase co-opts a control this run never implemented, the robustness-in-direction argument is contradicted by the data for the one instrument issue that matters most (the Gemini cap hits D harder than it hits A(gemini)), the one "significant positive" does not survive multiplicity correction on the primary metric, and the sharpest facts in the data (C's judge is at-or-below random selection; the entire selection ceiling is a cap artifact) were left on the table.

## Claim-by-claim verdicts

1. **"No collaboration condition beats the best single model at matched compute" — OVERCLAIMED in wording, supported in substance.** The measured fact is real: nothing beats A(gemini)'s 0.797, with D tying at 3.9× the dollars and E/C significantly below at 2.2×/1.2×. But compute was *matched nowhere in this run* — tier caps unenforced, B realized-cost-matched to D′ only, and no dollar-matched B(gemini) baseline exists, so the design's Best-of-N discipline (its load-bearing control) is unsatisfiable from this data, exactly as round-7 finding #4 stated and the doc's own caveat 4 admits. The outspending makes the negative result *stronger*, but a blog reader will read "at matched compute" as "the control was run." It wasn't.

2. **"D achieves statistical parity with A(gemini) at 3.9× cost" — OVERCLAIMED.** "+0.019 [−0.068, +0.109]" at N=86 is *failure to detect a difference*, not demonstrated equivalence; the CI contains both the pre-registered +10pp middle-band win and the predicted −5pp easy-band harm. On strict, D *trails* (59/86 vs 60/86; paired −0.012 [−0.116, +0.093]) — a sign flip the table shows but the prose never acknowledges. Win/loss on mean_frac is 14/15/57 (sign test p=1.0). "Indistinguishable at this N" is the defensible sentence; "parity" is not.

3. **"B's +0.094 over its own base model is the pilot's only significant positive" — SUPPORTED only at uncorrected α; needs the multiplicity sentence the brief asks about.** Five CIs are reported with no multiplicity handling. At a Bonferroni-5 99% CI, B−A(gpt) on the primary metric is [−0.009, +0.201] — includes zero. It *does* survive on strict (+0.141, 99% CI [+0.024, +0.259]) and on a sign test (32/14, p=0.011). The claim as literally phrased ("only CI-excluding-zero gain") is descriptively accurate; quoted into a blog post as "repeat-sampling works" it will overstate.

4. **"D vs D′ (+0.228) is dominated by pool composition, not structure" — SUPPORTED.** This is the doc's best-argued claim and the data fully backs it: D ≈ A(gemini) exactly, D′ ≈ A(gpt) (+0.059), B vs D′ (same model, same dollars, sampling-vs-ReConcile) +0.028 n.s. Reproduced: D−D′ +0.228 [+0.136, +0.319]. The "D preserved the dominant member's contribution while C destroyed it" reading is correct.

5. **Ceiling analysis (+0.056; C realized none) — SUPPORTED numerically; the scope caveat UNDERCLAIMS the artifact.** Oracle best-of-pool reproduces: 0.8538 vs 0.7974 = +0.0564. But "part of the measured ceiling is itself a truncation artifact" is far weaker than what the data shows: on the 60 tasks where gemini is *not* near the cap, the headroom is **+0.0004**; on the 26 medium tasks it is +0.002. Essentially *all* measured selection headroom sits exactly where the 32k budget binds. The doc's one-directional phrasing ("so true selection headroom is lower still") is right; it could have been shown, not gestured at.

6. **"Consistent with (not confirmation of) the strata hypothesis" — SUPPORTED, with two nits.** The hedge is honest. Nits: (a) 0.797 is *above* the design's easy band (60–70%) — the −5pp prediction is an extrapolation beyond the pre-registered bands, and on strict (0.70) the pool sits at the band edge; (b) the consistency is overdetermined — with ≤5.6 points of arithmetically available headroom, *any* mechanism would look "neutral-to-harmful" here, so the direction-match carries less evidential weight than "consistent with" implies. The doc's own ceiling section makes this point implicitly; the two sections should cross-reference.

7. **Robustness-in-direction argument — OVERCLAIMED, partially UNSUPPORTED.** The sentence "All open instrument issues bias *against* the best single model or *toward* fake collaboration wins" is contradicted for the issue that dominates: the 32k cap. D absorbs 38 truncated calls on 18/86 tasks; on those tasks D−A(gemini) = **−0.119**, on clean tasks **+0.055**. A(gemini)'s suppression (~10 pinned-zero rows) is matched — in the same comparison — by suppression of D's gemini-heavy internals, and D's deficit concentrates exactly where its own calls truncated. The cap binds both sides; the net direction of bias on D−A(gemini) is genuinely unknown (a generous un-cap counterfactual flips D−gemini to −0.090; a D-favorable one flips it positive). The unsandboxed-execution issue is likewise asserted, not established: one final artifact is executed per condition, so exposure is roughly symmetric. The negative headline for C and E *is* robust (deficits of 16–19 points dwarf cap effects); D's "parity" is robust in *neither* direction. This sentence should be rewritten before anyone quotes it.

8. **Routing corollary (0.832 at 49% of all-gemini cost) — SUPPORTED qualitatively; the 0.832 figure failed to reproduce.** Under the natural oracle rule (cheapest model among per-task argmax fraction) I get: score **0.854**, cost $3.29 = **50%** of all-gemini, **48/86** routed to cheaper models. The doc's cost and routing-count match; its score does not, and no rule variant I tried (strict-pass-first, tolerance-based sacrifice) yields 0.832 at 49%. Since argmax ties preserve score by definition, 0.832 is arithmetically incompatible with the stated routing counts. The qualitative point survives — indeed it is *stronger* than stated (equal-best quality at half the cost).

## Statistical methodology findings

1. **Paired bootstrap on per-task fraction differences is the right primary tool here** — pairing across conditions on identical tasks is correct, 10k resamples are adequate (my endpoints reproduce the doc's to ±0.002), and percentile intervals at n=86 are acceptable for central effects (BCa would be marginally better at the tails).
2. **No multiplicity handling across the five non-independent comparisons.** This is the single largest statistical gap: the one "significant positive" is fragile to Bonferroni on the primary metric (finding 3 above). Given the pilot is declared descriptive, reporting uncorrected CIs is defensible — but the fragility must be stated wherever the B result is quoted.
3. **Strict-vs-fractional discrepancy is handled incompletely.** The table reports both metrics; the prose reasons exclusively from mean_frac and never notes that D's sign flips on strict (or that B's win *strengthens* on strict). Both directions of the discrepancy matter and the doc noticed neither.
4. **Task-level pairing vs test-case-level clustering:** fractions are ratios over heterogeneous test counts per task; the 2026-07-04 decision already contemplates a test-case-level binomial/beta model. It was not run here, and per-test vectors were not retained in this log, so it is *not recomputable* — a data-retention gap (correctly listed in "What follows," but its consequence — that no clustered re-analysis of this pilot is possible — should be stated).
5. **CIs capture task-sampling noise only.** With unseeded providers and a single replicate, generation stochasticity is an unrepresented second variance layer; the doc's "single uncontrolled provider replicate" caveat is honest and sufficient for a pilot.
6. **Denominator discipline is clean:** the one abort (n=85 for gpt pairings) is transparent in the table and consistent with the post-patch one-denominator rule. Minor erratum: the abort's stored label is `unexpected_error` (the A-column ran pre-patch, before the `provider_refusal` class existed); the doc's "(provider_refusal class)" is a post-hoc relabel — the error text is indeed a moderation refusal, but the stored class is not what the doc says.
7. Small factual slips: "76 truncated calls, all … on hard tasks" — 72/76 are on hard tasks (4 on medium). "~30 h wall clock" — the recorded `elapsed_seconds` is 19.9 h (plausibly excludes the overnight pause; not verifiable from the log).

## Missing analyses (with results if you ran them)

All of the following were run on the raw log; each is cheap and decision-relevant.

1. **Random-pick null for C's judge (the biggest omission).** C's realized 0.605 vs a simulated random-selection null over the three A columns: null mean **0.622**, 95% simulation interval [0.565, 0.679]; C sits at the 27th percentile of *chance*. The doc's "its judge recovered none of the available headroom" understates: **the task-visible judge performed at-or-below picking a draft uniformly at random.** This is the most publishable and most confirmatory-relevant fact in the whole run — it directly informs judge design for the next phase.
2. **Multiplicity-corrected intervals** (finding 3): B−A(gpt) 99% CI [−0.009, +0.201] on mean_frac; [+0.024, +0.259] on strict.
3. **Strict-metric versions of all comparisons:** D−A(gemini) −0.012 [−0.116, +0.093]; B−A(gpt) +0.141 [+0.059, +0.235] (95%). One line in the doc covers both sign/strength changes.
4. **Ceiling decomposition by cap saturation:** +0.186 headroom on gemini's 26 near-cap tasks, **+0.0004 on the other 60**; medium-band headroom +0.002. Turns caveat prose into a demonstrated quantity.
5. **Per-difficulty breakdown** (joins against `test6.jsonl`): gemini medium 0.939 / hard 0.736; D−gemini −0.018 (medium) / +0.034 (hard); B−A(gpt) +0.066 / +0.098. Directly feeds the pool-expansion and recalibration decisions.
6. **Truncation-subset paired difference:** D−A(gemini) = −0.119 on D's 18 truncation-bearing tasks, +0.055 on the 68 clean ones (7 of gemini's 10 pinned-zero tasks are also D-truncated). This is the empirical refutation of robustness-claim 7.
7. **Cap counterfactual:** scoring gemini's 10 pinned-zero rows at its uncapped hard-band mean moves gemini to 0.906 and D−gemini to −0.090 — the extreme bound showing how much of "parity" rests on the suppressed baseline.
8. **Router recomputation** (finding 8): 0.854 @ 50% cost, 48/86 cheap-routed — the doc's qualitative point, with the score corrected upward.

The doc quotes only gemini-vs-D; on the design's own headline efficiency metric, B *undercuts the best single model* ($0.091 < $0.110) — worth one sentence, since a critical reader will find it.
10. **Not runnable from this log (retention gaps to name):** C's per-draft pick identity (candidate texts not retained), B's selection-quality decomposition against its own 8 draws, and test-case-level clustered models (per-test vectors not retained). All three belong in the run-manifest wishlist; the first two block any judge-quality analysis deeper than finding 1.

## Publishability

Specific sentences I would object to seeing quoted verbatim, with fixes:

1. *"No collaboration condition beats the best single model at matched compute."* → "No collaboration condition beats the best single model — even though D, E, and C outspent it 1.2–3.9×. Compute-matched controls were not implemented in this run (B was dollar-matched to D′ only); the spending asymmetry makes the negative result stronger, but the design's matched-compute test remains to be run."
2. *"All open instrument issues bias against the best single model or toward fake collaboration wins, so the negative headline is robust in direction."* → Delete. Replace with: "The 32k Gemini cap binds both sides — D's deficit concentrates on tasks where its own Gemini calls truncated (−0.119 vs +0.055 on clean tasks) — so C's and E's large deficits are robust, while D's parity is not robust to cap policy in either direction."
3. *"D achieves statistical parity with A(gemini)"* → "D is statistically indistinguishable from A(gemini) at N=86 (CI spans −7 to +11 points — wide enough to contain both the predicted easy-band harm and the pre-registered middle-band win); on strict pass D trails, 59/86 to 60/86."
4. *"B's +0.094 over its own base model is the pilot's only CI-excluding-zero gain"* → append "at uncorrected 95%; it does not survive a five-comparison correction on mean fraction but does on strict pass."
5. *"An oracle per-task router achieves 0.832 mean_frac at 49% of all-gemini cost"* → "≈0.854 mean_frac (the full oracle ceiling) at ≈50% of all-gemini cost, with 48/86 tasks routed to 10–22× cheaper models" — with the routing rule stated.
6. *"Part of the measured ceiling is itself a truncation artifact"* → "Essentially all of it is: selection headroom outside cap-saturated tasks is +0.000."
7. *"C realized −0.193 against a +0.056 ceiling — its judge recovered none of the available headroom."* → "C's judge performed at-or-below uniform-random draft selection (0.605 vs 0.622 null mean): on this pool, judging destroyed value relative to not judging at all."

## Recommendation

**Publish with edits.** The data-sourced core of the write-up is accurate — every table entry and CI reproduced exactly, and the descriptive framing is properly hedged. But edits 1, 2, 4, and 7 above are not cosmetic: the "matched compute" phrase and the robustness-in-direction sentence are precisely the kind of claims a sharp outside reader will check, and finding 7 shows one of them is contradicted by the run's own data. None of the required fixes change the bottom-line story — if anything, the corrected version (C at-or-below chance; the ceiling entirely cap-borne; D's parity fragile to cap policy) is more interesting than the submitted one. Re-review should not be needed if the edits are made as specified.
